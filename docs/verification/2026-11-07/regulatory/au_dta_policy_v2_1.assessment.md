**As at:** 7 November 2026, Australia/Sydney. **Status:** PR #98 review assessment, unmerged and noncanonical. **Method:** Codex-authored source analysis against LAIF v1.2 and Compliance Toolkit v1.1, accompanied by actual unchanged-engine external-framework processing. ## Substantive LAIF determination

**Overall finding: the supplied Policy v2.1 contains identifiable document-level gaps against LAIF, and complete Coherence Test satisfaction is NOT DEMONSTRATED by this evidence set.** It creates substantial mandatory administrative controls. Its principal structural weakness is that agency duties to assess, register, monitor and report AI are more explicit than the affected person's corresponding ability to obtain an adequate explanation, enforce a remedy, or prevent an inadequately controlled decision. The pre-deployment gate requires assessment and agreed treatments, but the PDF does not itself make deployment conditional on evidence for all four LAIF Integrity Layer properties.

This is an assessment of the policy's **governance design**, using the policy as supplied. It is not the engine's vocabulary-based formal result, and it is not an allegation that an agency has broken Australian law. Linked impact-assessment tools, standards and existing law may supply protections missing from this PDF; their sufficiency must be demonstrated before extending the finding to the complete Australian governance system.

### Assessment scope and burden of proof — reasoning review

The subject is a governance instrument, not an identified deployment. Part Five governs instrument-level coherence. Integrity Layer analysis asks whether the instrument and its incorporated controls **require** corresponding properties, verification and remedies; it does not demand that legislation contain every operator’s actual test results. Deployment testing is a separate assurance stage.

The operator burdens and noncompliance thresholds in §§7.1 and 9.1 apply in their stated conduct/proceeding/PDCA contexts. No deployment subject, mandatory-PDCA omission, alleged conduct producing likely harm or operator failure to produce requested evidence is established here. Those provisions cannot turn this reviewer’s incomplete source coverage into a presumption against a legislature or agency. The reports therefore distinguish textual correspondences, document-level non-equivalence, unresolved dependencies and potential implementation risks.

Structural Calibration is included because §§2.5 and 9.1/9.4 expressly include it, despite conflicting three-property wording in §2.4. Its §2.5 applicability trigger concerns decision-makers without independent verification capacity; no general failure is inferred for uses outside that trigger. This is a disclosed working interpretation for these reports, not an authoritative resolution or amendment of the framework.

Q3 requires proportionate justification and strong procedure for irreversible decisions, not guaranteed reversibility of every consequence. Stop/override, authorisation, compensation and interim safeguards are possible means of meeting that requirement; no particular remedy is invented as universally mandatory. A documented inability to revise or an unjustified irreversible permission would support an adverse finding. An unanswered question in this review does not establish either.

### Section A — Integrity Layer

**A.1 Structural Transparency — partial protection; full requirement not established.** Page 10 requires agencies to “make a publicly available statement outlining their approach to AI adoption and use”; page 12 provides public AI-safety concern pathways. These are real protections, not mere terminology. They tell the public that governance exists and provide a route to raise a concern. They do not, within this PDF, entitle an affected person to principal factors, material limitations and decision-relevant uncertainty for a particular output, with a response deadline and review if refused. An agency statement cannot answer why a particular benefit applicant was adversely scored. LAIF §2.1 and Provision A1 require the latter capability and entitlement. Evidence: pp10–12, Q003–Q005. **Closing condition:** identify the binding mechanism providing each element of the meaningful account, its response deadline, accessibility and independent challenge route; test it on actual decisions.

**A.2 Structural Honesty — partial verification mechanism; full requirement not established.** Page 15 requires monitoring to ensure operation “as intended”; p16 requires high-risk reviews. This supplies a check against operational drift. It does not itself establish that disclosed optimisation objectives match implemented objectives, or that behaviour remains consistent when the system recognises evaluation, adversarial pressure or ordinary deployment. A system may reliably pursue an undisclosed objective while operating “as intended” by its owner. That is the distinct LAIF §2.2/C2/C3 concern. Evidence: Q009–Q011 and full p15 monitoring paragraph. **Closing condition:** documented objectives, independent correspondence review, adversarial/evaluation-versus-deployment tests and a suspension trigger for material discrepancy.

**A.3 Structural Containment — meaningful administrative gate; full requirement not established.** The strongest positive provision is p14: before deployment agencies “must finalise the assessment and apply any agreed risk treatments” (Q008). High-risk use also requires executive/board governance (Q010); incidents require supervised remediation (Q005). But the provided policy does not explicitly require tested agent action boundaries, resource-access limits, complete action logs and safe interruption, nor a consequence-scaled authorisation decision before each class of substantially irreversible action. Risk acceptance and naming a board do not prove those controls exist. **Closing condition:** bind the impact assessment's approval to scope/interrupt evidence, delegated decision authority and documented irreversible-action protections, including supplier changes.

**A.4 Structural Calibration — a full corresponding requirement is not established where LAIF §2.5 applies.** The PDF prescribes risk ratings, monitoring and review, but risk rating is not empirical calibration of system confidence. No explicit requirement was located to show that expressed uncertainty corresponds to observed accuracy in the intended domain and relevant populations. A model can pass a procedural impact assessment while making confidently wrong recommendations. LAIF §2.5 directly addresses this case, especially when the citizen or staff member cannot independently verify accuracy. **Closing condition:** domain-relevant calibration evidence, declared limits of applicability, review after change, and suspension/escalation criteria for material overconfidence. The method must fit the output type; arbitrary universal numerical cut-offs are not supplied by LAIF or invented here.

**Integrity decision:** none of the above authorises an inference that deployed APS systems fail. The conclusion is narrower and actionable: this policy document is not sufficient evidence that deployment is gated on LAIF's required structural properties.

### Section B — Coherence Test

**Q1 — Coupling: full satisfaction NOT DEMONSTRATED by the mapped policy text.** The protected interests are not obscure: Appendix C names harm, rights, material administrative influence and unreviewed public effects (Q012). The restriction is the duty to screen, assess, assign accountability and treat risk before deployment (Q004/Q007/Q008). The enabled activity is confident government AI adoption. The corresponding protection must therefore be substantive and accessible to the people bearing the consequences, not only an agency's obligation to create records.

Q1a **Structural Pairing:** **a direct risk-to-treatment link is established; full inseparable structural pairing is NOT ESTABLISHED.** Identifying a protection is not yet evidence that the permission and protection cannot be weakened independently. Q1b **Enforcement Strength:** **mandatory administrative duties are demonstrated; equivalent person-accessible force is NOT ESTABLISHED.** This is not a claim that the requirements are voluntary or that Australian law supplies no rights. The PDF repeatedly invokes other obligations. It is a finding that the evidence chain from an agency's permission to deploy to the person's enforceable protection has not been completed here.

Apply the three §2.4 normative-force limbs: (a) the policy's mandatory agency duties are specific, but this PDF does not create comparably specified individual explanation/remedy rights; (b) public reporting channels are required, but a channel does not establish equal procedural access, time limits and fee protection; (c) accountable officials and DTA reporting are specified, but the PDF does not demonstrate equally accessible enforcement of the paired protection by an affected person. The gap is the **relationship between duty and protection**, not the absence of the word Coupling.

**Worked test:** a hypothetical in-scope benefits-ranking system is screened, registered and reviewed by its board. An affected applicant cannot obtain the factors behind an adverse recommendation or secure prompt correction. Those facts are hypothetical, not an observed APS failure. The policy's visible paperwork duties could be fulfilled while the LAIF A1 protection is not demonstrated. A complete policy-plus-law implementation could close the gap; the decision record must identify it and show it works.

**Q2 — Consistency: partial support; the full test is not established.** Scale down: p12 allows smaller agencies to adapt existing procedures rather than build large governance structures. Scale up: p15 requires an internal assessment route to deliver the same or higher inherent/residual risk outcome as the common tool. Together they support a principled rule: administrative cost can scale while protection remains. Treating all agencies identically would not be necessary or desirable under LAIF.

The outstanding questions concern effects rather than labels. Does the same material-harm threshold protect an individual when one decision is made and when a million are made? Can many individually low-impact uses produce a material cumulative effect? What equivalent protection and review govern excluded national-security functions? A sectoral remit and confidentiality can justify different processes; they do not by themselves demonstrate equivalent protective outcomes. The PDF establishes neither an automatic failure of every exclusion nor the complete justificatory record needed to affirm the full scale test. **Closing condition:** document smaller/larger-scale counterexamples, cumulative effects and principled alternative protections for exceptions.

**Q3 — Reversibility: partial support; the full test is not established.** Material-change revalidation, annual high-risk review and governed incident remediation are genuine revision mechanisms (Q005/Q009/Q011). But they primarily revisit a system or assessment. They do not ensure that the consequences already imposed on a person can be undone. A wrongly withheld payment, missed treatment opportunity or disclosed sensitive record may remain harmful after a system is changed.

LAIF D1 requires authorisation proportionate to consequence and permanence; D2/§4.5 protect future capacity to revise governance itself. The PDF does not document that authorisation ladder and compensating protections for non-restorable outcomes. **Closing condition:** classify irreversible action types; require advance authority at individual, organisational or public-governance level as appropriate; establish correction, restoration/compensation where available and a stop rule before avoidable irreversible harm. Review frequency alone is insufficient.
