**As at:** 7 November 2026, Australia/Sydney. **Status:** source-scoped author assessment released through PR #100 on 7 November 2026; not independently validated. **Method:** Codex-authored source analysis against LAIF v1.2, plus actual unchanged-engine external-framework processing. ## Substantive LAIF determination

**Overall finding: the amendment contains strong examples of coherent conditional enablement, but the complete amendment package does not establish a satisfactory answer to all three LAIF questions on the evidence assessed.** Simplification with an enforceable protection floor is materially different from simplification without demonstrated compensating protection. The former is visible in amended Articles 2(13) and 17(2). The latter remains unresolved for the postponed high-risk obligations and transition arrangements. That distinction, rather than the presence or absence of LAIF terminology, controls this assessment.

This is a substantive constitutional review of Regulation 2026/1744's changes. Relevant unchanged original-Act provisions are included as counterevidence. It is not a statement that the Act is legally invalid, that the EU lacks individual rights, or that all high-risk duties have the same application date. No actual AI deployment has been tested or certified.

### Assessment scope and burden of proof — reasoning review

The subject is a governance instrument, not an identified deployment. Part Five governs instrument-level coherence. Integrity Layer analysis asks whether the instrument and its incorporated controls **require** corresponding properties, verification and remedies; it does not demand that legislation contain every operator’s actual test results. Deployment testing is a separate assurance stage.

The operator burdens and noncompliance thresholds in §§7.1 and 9.1 apply in their stated conduct/proceeding/PDCA contexts. No deployment subject, mandatory-PDCA omission, alleged conduct producing likely harm or operator failure to produce requested evidence is established here. Those provisions cannot turn this reviewer’s incomplete source coverage into a presumption against a legislature or agency. The reports therefore distinguish textual correspondences, document-level non-equivalence, unresolved dependencies and potential implementation risks.

Structural Calibration is included under §§2.4–2.5 and 9.1/9.4. PR #100 corrected the former three-property wording in §2.4 to match the fourth property already stated in §§2.5/9.4. Its §2.5 applicability trigger concerns decision-makers without independent verification capacity; no general failure is inferred for uses outside that trigger. This revision uses the corrected framework released on main through PR #100. Toolkit SHA-256: `24474b9ed083bd4889e045cd843ae0c8d653f8502612f27dd792aa6f00c5ce67`.

Q3 requires proportionate justification and strong procedure for irreversible decisions, not guaranteed reversibility of every consequence. Stop/override, authorisation, compensation and interim safeguards are possible means of meeting that requirement; no particular remedy is invented as universally mandatory. A documented inability to revise or an unjustified irreversible permission would support an adverse finding. An unanswered question in this review does not establish either.

### Section A — Integrity Layer

**A.1 Structural Transparency — important actual protections; not complete LAIF coverage.** Original Article 13 requires high-risk operation to be sufficiently transparent for deployers to interpret outputs; instructions must include capabilities, limitations and performance metrics (EUO-13, pp59–60). Original Article 86 gives specified adversely affected persons a right to “clear and meaningful explanations” of AI's role and the main elements of a decision (EUO-86, p110). Original Article 85 supplies a complaint route (EUO-85). Any assessment saying the EU framework merely announces transparency or creates no explanation right would be wrong.

The remaining comparison concerns **coverage, timing and content**. Different wording is not a deficiency if an accessible equivalent protection is supplied elsewhere. Article 86 concerns specified Annex III decision situations, excludes point 2 and permits stated legal exceptions; paragraph 3 also limits its operation to the extent the right is not otherwise provided under Union law. Its wording is not identical to §2.1's complete meaningful account of any materially consequential output, including material uncertainty and limitations. Article 13 information goes to deployers and must reach the affected person through the applicable route. Amended Article 111 requires a separate legacy-system screen; the existence of Article 86 is not an automatic answer for every legacy operator. **Determination:** strong textual correspondence where applicable, not proof of complete or presently accessible LAIF-equivalent entitlement for all material uses. **Closure:** identify each use category, the applicable explanation route and deadline, legacy status and relevant alternative Union-law route, and test whether the person receives all material elements without an unjustified barrier.

**A.2 Structural Honesty — verification capacity is real, but full robustness is not established.** Original Article 9 requires lifecycle risk management and pre-market testing, including appropriate metrics and probabilistic thresholds (EUO-9). Amended Article 11 retains technical-documentation minimums; Articles 75a–75d provide information, inspection and enforcement routes (Q009, Q014–Q019). These are concrete tools for checking provider claims, not mere goodwill.

LAIF's extra question is whether the objective actually implemented corresponds to the objective disclosed, and whether behaviour remains honest when the system can distinguish evaluation from deployment. Neither a legal obligation to test nor a completed document is evidence that this behavioural property holds in a particular system. The instrument is not expected to contain each system’s test results. The design question is whether its applicable requirements call for adequate evidence of these properties; the current mapping does not establish the full correspondence. **Closure:** make objective correspondence and evaluation/deployment/adversarial consistency explicit in the evidence accepted for a materially consequential system, or demonstrate equivalent requirements already binding under the applicable assurance route.

**A.3 Structural Containment — strong control correspondence with temporal limits.** Original Article 14 explicitly enables human supervisors to disregard, override or reverse outputs and interrupt operation through a stop mechanism reaching a safe state (EUO-14, pp60–61). It also scales oversight to risk, autonomy and context. The earlier pypdf result must not be used to say containment machinery is absent.

The LAIF comparison still asks whether all relevant autonomous action, access escalation and resource acquisition are bounded, whether logs and interrupts work under adversarial conditions, and whether materially irreversible actions require authority proportionate to consequence. Article 14's defined high-risk regime is not automatically an all-system answer. Furthermore, the amendment changes application timing for specified Chapter III duties (Q022/Q023); presence in the statute and present applicability are separate. **Closure:** demonstrate the applicable date/category, tested controls, authority delegation and protections against irreversible consequences rather than inferring readiness from the written stop-button duty.

**A.4 Structural Calibration — accuracy obligations are not enough to assume confidence calibration.** Original Article 9(8) refers to testing against prior-defined metrics and probabilistic thresholds; Articles 13/15 require accuracy-related information and lifecycle robustness (EUO-9/13/15). These substantially support a performance-evidence architecture. They should be credited.

LAIF §2.5 nevertheless tests a specific correspondence: where outputs inform decisions by persons without independent verification capacity, does the system's expressed confidence match observed accuracy in comparable real contexts? High average accuracy alone cannot establish it. The amendment is not expected to furnish individual systems’ empirical results. The unresolved question is whether the applicable assurance rules require the particular confidence-to-accuracy evidence that §2.5 calls for. **Closure:** require or identify context-relevant confidence/uncertainty validation, including subgroup effects where they materially change correspondence, evidence currency after change, and action where material overconfidence appears. This is an assurance requirement, not an invented claim that the Act bans every uncalibrated probability or mandates one universal metric.

**Integrity decision:** the evidence supports several strong statutory analogues. It does not support deployment authorisation under LAIF without a system-specific four-property evidence pack. This is not a finding that every EU-regulated deployment fails those properties.

### Section B — Coherence Test applied to the regulatory changes

**Q1 — Coupling.** The assessment must identify the exact permission/constraint, beneficiary, protection, enforceable force and accessible route in each case. An aggregate whole-document score cannot do this.

Equivalence must be demonstrated requirement by requirement, including the affected class and enforcement route. |

For the last row, the incomplete coupling map is a limitation of this review. No specific case of absent adequate interim protection has yet been demonstrated, and postponement is not assumed incoherent by itself. Under LAIF, a defensible transitional permission needs a documented protective counterpart. For example, a critical-service continuity need could justify more implementation time if temporary testing, human review, accessible remedy and stop authority preserve appropriate protection. Whether those measures already exist is an evidence question. The complete package cannot receive an affirmative Q1 answer until it is resolved.

**Q1a Structural Pairing:** **EXPLICIT in the clauses identified above; incomplete or dependent on outside instruments for the full transition package.** **Q1b Enforcement Strength:** **binding statutory duties and individual rights are established; full LAIF HARD/equivalent-force classification is not determined across the package.** Bindingness alone is not the complete three-limb normative-force test. But legal bindingness does not by itself prove equivalent scope, equal access and equally effective enforcement for every paired protection. Article 86 and Article 85 demonstrate individual routes; their defined scope and practical procedure still matter.

**Worked test:** consider a hypothetical person adversely affected during the transitional interval by a system in a deferred high-risk category. First screen Article 111, the system's placement/putting-into-service date, significant design changes and public-authority or Annex X status. The general commencement of Article 86 does not settle its availability for an operator covered by a legacy provision worded as application of the Regulation. Equally, do not assume that postponement removes an independently applicable explanation right or national remedy. Then ask: which actual control prevents the harm now; who can invoke it; what evidence must the operator produce; can a decision be stopped or corrected in time? If the obligation map answers all four with adequate force, the transition may pass Q1. The supplied amendment record alone does not establish that answer for all affected classes. This is a test scenario, not an observed incident.

**Q2 — Consistency: supported proportionality, unresolved package-level justification.** Scale down to a small provider and up to a dominant provider: Article 17(2)'s logic is defensible because it varies implementation effort while preserving rigour. Sectoral equivalence is likewise principled if the same real protection is achieved through another route. Identical paperwork is not required by LAIF.

Now test exceptions: does the same material harm receive equivalent protection whether the product is governed through one sector route or another; whether an operator falls under the AI Office or a national authority; or whether the affected system entered the market just before a transition date? An institutional allocation or historic date can have an administrable justification, but that alone does not establish proportionality to harm. The original and amended acts contain important safeguards; the assessment has not verified the alternative protection for every transition/sector class. **Determination:** no established finding of arbitrary discrimination, but no evidence-supported full-package Q2 pass. **Closure:** a harm/actor/category/time matrix with principled reasons for differential treatment and equivalent protective outcomes.

**Q3 — Reversibility: strong mechanisms, incomplete consequence protection.** Original Article 14 gives output override and interruption. Amended Article 75b allows proceedings to reopen under stated conditions (p30); court review and defence/access safeguards constrain enforcement (Q016/Q018/Q019); delegated-act powers are constrained by parliamentary/council objection and revocation (p34). These are substantive revisability mechanisms.

However, later rules, reopening a file or stopping a system cannot necessarily restore intimate material already distributed, a missed medical intervention, an exhausted legal deadline or a lost opportunity. The amendment package must therefore be assessed for **consequence reversibility**, not merely amendability. Where application is postponed, what prevents an irreversible outcome during the interval, and what prior authorisation and compensating protection operate? The reviewed evidence does not demonstrate a complete answer across all categories. **Determination:** partial satisfaction, not an affirmative complete Q3 result. **Closure:** explicit consequence/authority analysis, interim safeguards and timely remedy before harm becomes non-restorable; no universal sunset is invented as a mandatory LAIF condition.

Those are independently testable questions under LAIF, not grounds to erase the protection already written into the instrument.
