# How LAIF assesses governance

LAIF examines the structure expressed in a document. It looks for specific obligations, protected interests, accountable actors, oversight, evidence, review and routes to correct decisions. Its findings and scores are deterministic diagnostics for human review.

## The Coherence Test

### Coupling

Does a restriction identify and protect the specific human interest at risk? Under LAIF, the protection needs normative force equivalent to the restriction. A reviewer should be able to identify the affected people, the interest being protected, the control and the responsibility for applying it.

### Consistency

Would the same governance reasoning remain just and workable across comparable actors and at smaller and larger scales? This question tests whether a rule rests on a coherent principle rather than an unexplained exception.

### Reversibility

Can future actors revise the arrangement and correct its consequences? Reviewers examine the capacity to amend rules, suspend use, reverse decisions and address effects that cannot simply be undone.

## The Integrity Layer

| Property | What the reviewer examines |
|---|---|
| Structural Transparency | Whether the basis for outputs, uncertainty and material limitations can be explained |
| Structural Honesty | Whether stated objectives correspond to implementation and behaviour |
| Structural Containment | Whether the system stays within documented boundaries and triggers authorisation for materially irreversible actions |

A document can specify these protections. Demonstrating their operation requires evidence from the relevant deployment.

## Self-Application

The governance actors themselves are subject to the framework's questions. Responsibilities, restrictions and correction mechanisms should also make sense for the institution writing or enforcing the rules.

## Two assessment channels

**External framework diagnostic assessment** examines substance in the source document's own vocabulary. It is intended for laws, standards, policies, supplier submissions and operational governance documents. A different vocabulary is not itself evidence of a missing safeguard.

**LAIF-native certification** is the framework's strict internal channel for documents that explicitly claim or seek LAIF adoption. Its PASS/FAIL result is scoped to LAIF criteria. It is not regulatory approval or external accreditation.

Remediation and patch guidance propose clauses, controls and evidence for the reviewer to consider. Recommendations must be assessed in the relevant institutional and legal context before adoption.

## Traceability and review

The toolkit records source identity and generates quoted evidence, gap registers, failure pathways and control recommendations. Automatic document-type and sector classification is heuristic. Verify both classifications and read quotations in their source context.

A gap identified in one document may be addressed by a separate procedure or control artefact. Conversely, a well-written provision may not be implemented. The review should distinguish what the document states, what it leaves unspecified and what operational evidence demonstrates.

Scores express the implemented rubric; they are not statistical confidence levels or legal conclusions. LAIF does not determine legal validity or claim authority over external jurisdictions or institutions.
