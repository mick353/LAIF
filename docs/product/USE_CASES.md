# Where LAIF can help

**Plain-English guide — 7 November 2026.** LAIF helps a reviewer answer:
“What protects people here, who must act, what evidence supports that claim,
and what happens when the decision needs to change?”

Its practical output is a source-linked review, evidence requests and proposed
controls. An analyst can attach substantive findings against the Integrity
Layer, Coupling, Consistency, Reversibility and Self-Application. Software checks
that references match the source; the analyst remains responsible for reasoning.

## Uses across fields

These are potential applications of the existing document-review workflow.
A sector profile supplies review context; it does not establish specialist
validation in that field. The whole-source development corpus covers public
policy, AI frameworks, regulation and a clinical procurement form. It does not
supply operational results for all fields below.

| Field | Example question | Useful inputs and output | Support today and expansion needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Government services | Can a person challenge an AI-assisted decision about them? | Agency policy, decision procedure and complaints process → identify stated protections and request missing evidence | Government-service profile; DTA policy exercised. A real service's outcomes and remedies still need field evidence. |
| Public-sector AI development | Who can approve a release, stop it and reverse effects? | Development standard, assessment and approval procedure → release review questions and proposed gates | Departmental-development profile; operational release testing must be supplied. |
| Health care | What must be shown before a clinical assistant is used? | Safety case, risk assessment and manufacturer form → evidence requests tied to clinical context | Clinical profile; full DTAC form exercised. Patient safety and system performance are not established by document processing. |
| Procurement | Do the supplier's claims match the buyer's requirements? | Tender, contract and supplier response → questions for due diligence and conditional controls | Procurement profile and document-type distinctions. Comparing several documents as one obligation set is an expansion. |
| Employment | Can an applicant or employee understand and challenge an AI-assisted decision? | Hiring policy, supplier submission and review route → source-linked scrutiny of interests and remedies | Employment profile; representative field cases and independent review remain needed. |
| Education | Can staff correct an automated marking or student-risk decision? | Assessment policy, system guide and appeal procedure → questions about review, correction and affected students | Education profile; actual institutional records and user testing remain needed. |
| Banking and insurance | What controls an AI-assisted credit, claims or fraud decision? | Model policy, operating procedure and escalation rules → questions about authority, review and correction | Financial-services profile; specialist legal and operational assessment remains needed. |
| Customer services | When must a chatbot hand a case to a person? | Support policy, escalation instructions and incident records → check stated handover and correction arrangements | General-governance path; scenario tests and service records are additional evidence. |
| Workplace software and privacy | What stops an assistant from disclosing restricted information? | Access policy, authorised-use rules and test records → distinguish stated boundaries from verified controls | General-governance and evidence-assurance paths; LAIF does not execute security tests itself. |
| Research and policy comparison | What protections differ, and which conclusions are uncertain? | Dated, source-hashed instruments → attributed findings, alternatives and disagreement records | Scholarly reporting and comparison tools exist. Independent reviewers and a held-out study are still required. |
| Autonomous agents and robotics | Who can interrupt an action before it becomes irreversible? | Capability tests, containment evidence and release records → documentary assurance questions | Optional frontier-safety evidence checks. Live agent or robot containment tests are not performed by LAIF. |
| Internal audit and quality assurance | Can a reviewer follow a conclusion back to its evidence? | Policy, evidence manifest and review record → inspect source identity, exact quotations and unresolved claims | Local provenance and evidence checks exist. Audit acceptance and control effectiveness need external evidence. |

## Where the strongest additional value may be

These are development hypotheses, not claims of a world-first invention or
measured superiority over another assurance method.

| Direction | Practical benefit | Current position | What would establish the value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connect each restriction to its specific protection and remedy | Expose a rule that controls people without an equivalent protection | LAIF's Coupling method and attributed findings support this reasoning; automatic retrieval does not prove the relationship | Independently reviewed provision-level cases, including cases where LAIF should accept the protection |
| Connect policy → contract → procedure → test → incident → remedy | Show whether a promise survives into the real decision process | Separate evidence files and local assurance checks exist; an integrated relationship model is not implemented | Traceable multi-document cases; deliberately broken links; proof that reviewers find more material defects |
| Review changes over time | Show what protection a new version adds, removes or postpones | Source versions and hashes are recorded; the DTA/EU work includes authored change analysis | Reusable provision mapping, effective-date handling and reviewed change reports across more instruments |
| Separate retrievable evidence from justified conclusions | Reduce confident reports based on an isolated sentence | Exact spans, table context, alternatives, explicit unassessed criteria and attributed reviews are implemented | Blind expert review of false positives, missed protections, misleading context and appropriate abstentions |
| Test governance across actors and scales, including the regulator | Find inconsistent rules and unaccountable rule makers | Consistency and Self-Application are framework questions; substantive judgements remain analyst work | Matched cases with principled exceptions and independent disagreement analysis |
| Test irreversible effects and confidence claims | Ask whether a stop mechanism can prevent the harm and whether confidence is calibrated | Integrity Layer and Reversibility guide review; optional evidence checks accept supplied records | Real action traces, stop/rollback tests, comparable-context calibration data and external review |

## The next quality step

The highest-value next step is an independent comparison study using unfamiliar
real documents, including strong controls, ambiguous provisions, templates and
contradictory evidence. Freeze the cases and scoring protocol before reviewers
see results. Compare LAIF-assisted review with a checklist and ordinary keyword
search. Measure missed protections, unsupported findings, review time and whether
a non-specialist can find the supporting evidence. Record disagreements before
adjudication. Existing study tooling supports this work; no such study has yet
established LAIF's accuracy or advantage.

Run it on local source files without paid model APIs or GitHub Actions. Add live
system evidence only as a separately scoped test. Do not replace the framework
or change thresholds to make a benchmark score look better.
